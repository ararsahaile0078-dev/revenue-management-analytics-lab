01

Forecasting as a commercial system

A forecast is not simply a number. It is an analytical assumption about future demand that influences inventory, pricing, capacity, revenue and profitability decisions.

Forecasting framework

Compare the forecast.

Different forecasting approaches produce different commercial interpretations. The objective is not simply accuracy — it is understanding how the forecast changes the decision.

01

Naive

Uses the most recent observed demand as the forecast. Simple, transparent and useful as a benchmark.

02

Moving Average

Smooths recent demand observations and reduces the influence of short-term fluctuations.

03

Weighted Average

Places greater importance on the most recent demand observations.

04

Exponential Smoothing

Updates the forecast progressively as new demand observations become available.

Revenue Management economics

From forecast to profit.

A demand forecast becomes commercially useful when it is connected to revenue, cost, capacity and profitability.

KPI Meaning Commercial use
Forecast demand Expected passenger demand. Supports inventory and capacity decisions.
Load factor Forecast demand relative to capacity. Indicates utilization pressure.
Total revenue Passenger and ancillary revenue. Measures commercial value generated.
Total cost Variable passenger cost plus flight cost. Establishes the economic cost base.
Profitability Revenue minus total cost. Shows whether the scenario creates economic value.
Profit margin Profit as a percentage of revenue. Helps evaluate commercial quality.
Break-even demand Passenger volume required to cover fixed costs. Indicates minimum viable demand.
RASM Revenue per available seat. Measures revenue productivity of capacity.
Yield Revenue generated per passenger. Connects demand to pricing performance.
01

Stimulate demand

When forecast demand is weak and load factor is below break-even, commercial stimulation may be required.

02

Protect margin

When demand is strong but profitability deteriorates, the focus should shift from volume to economic quality.

03

Optimize price

When capacity becomes constrained, stronger demand can support tighter inventory controls and higher fares.

Commercial interpretation

Forecasting is a decision system.

When demand is weak

  • 1. Stimulate demand where appropriate.
  • 2. Monitor break-even load factor.
  • 3. Avoid adding capacity without demand support.
  • 4. Protect contribution margin.

When demand is strong

  • 1. Protect higher-value inventory.
  • 2. Monitor capacity constraints.
  • 3. Optimize price and yield.
  • 4. Maximize profitable revenue.
