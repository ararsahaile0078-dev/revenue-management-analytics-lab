Demand becomes decision.

Explore how airline demand forecasts are constructed, tested and translated into Revenue Management decisions. Change the assumptions, compare forecasting methods and observe how forecast quality affects commercial interpretation.

01

Forecasting as a commercial system

A forecast is not simply a number. It is an analytical assumption about future demand that influences inventory, pricing and capacity decisions.

Forecasting framework

Four ways to read demand.

Different forecasting approaches make different assumptions about the future. The purpose of this laboratory is to make those assumptions visible.

01 / NAIVE

Naive Forecast

Uses the latest observed demand as the forecast for the next period. Simple, transparent and useful as a baseline.

02 / MOVING AVERAGE

Moving Average

Smooths recent observations to reduce short-term noise and create a more stable demand estimate.

03 / EXPONENTIAL

Exponential Smoothing

Gives greater weight to recent observations while retaining information from earlier demand.

04 / TREND

Trend Forecast

Extends the observed direction of demand into the future. Useful when the market is systematically growing or declining.

Booking curve

Demand arrives through time.

Airline demand is observed progressively before departure. Forecasting therefore requires analysts to distinguish observed bookings from the demand that has not yet materialised.
